# Image sources and licences

Every image on the site, where it comes from, and on what terms. Checked 9 November 2026; reference works R014–R024 added 10 November 2026.

## Rules

- **Reference works, not the collection.** All object photographs show objects held by a museum, not by MURANIQ.
  Every page that shows one says so ("Reference work. Held by …"), links the museum record, and offers no
  enquiry or sale. The contact page and `/api/contact/` refuse enquiries about these objects.
- **Licence: CC0 only.** Every photograph below was released by the holding museum under Creative Commons Zero
  (no rights reserved). We credit the museum anyway, on the image and in the dossier.
- **No implied endorsement.** The Cleveland Museum of Art asks open-access users not to suggest the museum
  endorses them. The site names the museum only as the holder of the object.
- **Text.** Cleveland's catalogue data is also CC0. The Art Institute of Chicago releases its descriptive text
  under CC BY 4.0, not CC0, so none of its text is reused: only facts (title, date, place, accession number)
  and the CC0 photograph. The Metropolitan Museum of Art releases its Open Access images and object data as
  CC0; its catalogue essays are not reused. Descriptions of Met and AIC objects describe only what the
  photograph shows and the facts in the record.
- **No necklaces yet.** No complete Venetian glass necklace with a public-domain (CC0) photograph was found in
  the Met, Cleveland or Chicago open-access collections on 10 November 2026; beads and bead-making scenes are
  shown instead.
- **No AI images.** Nothing here is generated, retouched or recomposed. The blurred backdrop behind some
  photographs is a decorative, `aria-hidden` copy of the same photograph, shown with the original whole on top.
- **Processing.** Downloaded from the museum's open-access image server, resized to at most 2200 px on the long
  side, converted to sRGB JPEG, metadata stripped. Not cropped, recoloured or altered otherwise.
- **Before launch** these reference works are to be replaced by MURANIQ's own photographs of its own objects.

### Further maker photographs (added 10 November 2026)

At most three photographs per maker. Found by searching the open-access APIs of the Met, the Cleveland Museum of
Art and the Art Institute of Chicago for every published maker. A photograph was kept only if:

- the museum marks it public domain or CC0;
- the museum record itself names that maker or firm;
- the date fits the maker.

Homonyms were rejected by hand, for example drawings by the painter Francesco Salviati and a "Antonio Seguso" who
cannot be matched to the right person. The museum's own attribution wording is shown in the caption. The search
found no free photographs for twentieth-century makers (Venini, Scarpa, Barovier & Toso and others), because their
works are still in copyright. Those makers are linked to museum records only.
